Sunlight contains all visible wavelengths of light, which travel straight toward Earth when it reaches us during the day. During daytime, most atmospheric molecules absorb certain wavelengths of light and reflect them back into space, while shorter blue wavelengths scatter more easily than longer ones (a phenomenon known as Rayleigh scattering). As the sun sets, it moves below the horizon where scattered blue light disappears into the atmosphere, allowing the longer wavelengths like red to pass through the remaining layers of air unobscured. This process leaves only reddish hues behind when observing the setting sun.
