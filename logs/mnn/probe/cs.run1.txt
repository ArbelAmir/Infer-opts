During daylight hours, sunlight travels directly through our atmosphere to reach the observer's eyes, scattering short-wavelength colors more than long ones based on Rayleigh scattering; because blue and violet light has very little wavelength, it scatters most efficiently from the sun's rays. When the Sun sets near the horizon, its light must travel through much greater amounts of atmosphere compared to daytime conditions before reaching us.
